## Legal notice (fixed wording)

Generic, naming no maker, and it says independent and non-commercial (never "open source": the licence is PolyForm Noncommercial). The device itself is named in full where it is chosen or connected.

- en: opentoys is an independent, non-commercial project, unaffiliated with any device maker. No device maker endorses or sponsors it. Product names and brands belong to their owners and only identify compatible hardware.
- zh-Hant: opentoys 獨立開發，屬非商業性質，與裝置製造商無關，也未獲其認可或贊助。產品名稱和品牌屬各自擁有者，僅用於標明支援的裝置。
- zh-Hans: opentoys 是独立的非商业项目，与设备制造商无关联，也未获其认可或赞助。产品名称和品牌归各自所有者所有，仅用于标明兼容设备。

## Style (every message, every language)

Short plain sentences, the useful thing first, one idea per sentence. No semicolons (`npm run check` fails on
; or ；): write two sentences. No "no X, no Y, no Z" lists, no dashes for asides, no filler ("simply", "just").
A colon only between a label and its value ("Elapsed: 0:09"), not to lead into an explanation. Don't repeat a
list of nouns (settings, saved patterns, history) from screen to screen.

## Coyote pattern names

Named after what they do, like the ring's. Three of the library's English names lean on sound and on the raw
protocol value, which runs the other way from the pulse rate ("Rising tone" slows down). The app calls them:
`rising-tone` Pulses slow down / 脈衝漸慢 / 脉冲渐慢, `falling-tone` Pulses speed up / 脈衝漸快 / 脉冲渐快,
`step-in` Slowing in steps / 逐級減慢 / 逐级减慢. Descriptions speak of strength (力度) and of pulses per second.
"Pulse" is kept for the device's pulses: a pattern's on-off rhythm is "bursts" (一開一關 / 一开一关). A unit
test works each pattern's direction out from its waveform and fails if the text disagrees.

## Pattern names

Hybrid: short names that already say what they do stay (Steady, Slow pulse, Heartbeat, Wave,
Breath, Staircase…); vague ones are named after what they output (e.g. Tingle bed → "Vibration waves + steady
e-stim" / 震動波浪＋固定電擊 / 振动波浪＋恒定电击; Session arc → "Timed session" / 定時模式 / 定时模式). In
combined patterns the name says which channel does what. Sensation words (tingle, 酥麻) may describe a feel in a
description, never replace a channel name.
